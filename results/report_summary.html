What the checks show. Springs now deliver Walton's tangential impulse during a collision; the earlier spring-only friction law is kept for comparison.

  • Friction now matches Walton at every impact angle. At k = 1e8 the largest difference in outgoing tangential over normal speed is 0.001, for μ = 0.25, 0.5 and 1. The spring alone gave −0.04 where Walton gives −0.70.
  • At working stiffness, grazing collisions still drift. At k = 1e6 and 20 kicks per contact time the largest difference is 0.014 at closing speed 0.1, 0.16 at speed 1 and 0.71 at speed 4, all at the most grazing angle. The softer contact lasts long enough for the line of centres to turn.
  • Normal contact follows the spring-dashpot laws. Contact time is within 2% and peak overlap within 0.4% of the law from k = 1e4 to 1e8.
  • Restitution needs a fine time step. At 50 kicks per contact time it matches the paper's e(v) within 0.6%. At 20 it is up to 3.2% too bouncy (0.722 against 0.700). The layer runs use 20.
  • Sustained friction is exact. A sphere set sliding on the plate ends rolling at 5/7 of its speed, on the predicted line throughout.
  • Where the hard engine runs, the hybrid converges to it. At Γ = 3.5, f* = 0.5, mean height is 4.517 hard, 4.555 at k = 1e5, 4.524 at 1e6 and 4.518 at 1e7, with seed scatter about 0.004. The new friction law changed these by less than the scatter.
  • Where the hard engine stalls, every hybrid run finishes. At Γ = 5, f* = 0.3, 10 layers, the hard engine used 300 million events in one twentieth of a cycle before cycle 3 at all three seeds. All 15 runs with springs completed 120 cycles.
  • There the answer depends on stiffness up to about k = 1e7, then is flat. Mean height is 23.77, 23.68, 23.50, 23.45 and 23.43 at k = 1e5 to 1e9, with seed scatter 0.03 to 0.08. Kinetic energy per sphere is 14.9 to 15.2 from k = 1e6 up; at k = 1e9 the three seeds give 15.17, 15.18 and 15.19.
  • The dependence is softness, not the time step. Rerunning at 50 kicks per contact time left height unchanged at k = 1e5, 1e6 and 1e7.
  • Stiffness costs time. CPU per run is 112, 225, 497, 949 and 1254 seconds from k = 1e5 to 1e9; largest overlap falls from 6.8% to 0.05% of a diameter.
  • The layer at this condition is not flat. One run shows a standing wave about 50 D long that flips between impacts, with the layer landing every second plate period.
  • Energy books improved. Unaccounted energy at 20 kicks per contact time is now a loss of 1e-3 to 2e-3 of throughput at the stalling condition, about half what it was. Making springs only below speed 0.1 still gains 1.6e-3.
  • A flat, hard-landing condition gives the same picture, more cleanly. At Γ = 5, f* = 0.6 the layer is a flat slab that lands every second cycle with a peak plate force of 29 times its weight. The hard engine stalled before cycle 3 at all three seeds; all 15 hybrid runs completed 160 cycles. Mean height is 9.449, 9.374, 9.318, 9.327 and 9.315 at k = 1e5 to 1e9, flat from 1e7, with seed scatter about 0.01.
  • An independent method agrees there. Hard spheres with the position nudge onto the plate (no springs) completed 6 of 6 runs at the flat condition, with mean height 9.308 ± 0.020 and kinetic energy 3.748 ± 0.006. The hybrid at k = 1e7 gives 9.318 and 3.750. Pushed starts did not get the plain hard engine through (0 of 12).
